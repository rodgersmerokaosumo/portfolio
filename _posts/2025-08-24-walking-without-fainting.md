*"But those who wait for the Lord [who expect, look for, and hope in Him] shall change and renew their strength and power; they shall lift up their wings and mount up [close to God] as eagles [mount up to the sun]; they shall run and not be weary, they shall walk and not faint or become tired." - Isaiah 40:31 (Amplified)*

Sometimes God's promises find you in the most unexpected places. For me, it is being surrounded by the weight of bad news after bad news about a beloved family member's illness. In those moments when your world feels like it's crumbling one phone call at a time, you discover what it really means to need strength that isn't your own.

## When Eagles Feel Like Walking

Isaiah 40:29-31 has become my lifeline during this season. These verses paint a beautiful picture of God's promise to renew our strength, describing it in three powerful images: soaring like eagles, running without weariness, and walking without fainting. 

When I first read these verses years ago, I'll admit I was drawn to the dramatic imagery of eagles' wings. Who wouldn't want to soar above their problems? But life has a way of teaching us that sometimes the greatest miracle isn't the ability to soar—it's the grace to keep walking when everything in you wants to give up.

Right now, I'm in the "walking without fainting" season. And I'm learning that this isn't somehow less than the eagle's wings promise—it's exactly what I need.

## The God Who Has Always Been There

What strikes me most about Isaiah 40 is its context. This chapter marks a shift in Isaiah's message from judgment to comfort. God is speaking to people facing exile, uncertainty, and loss—people who needed to hear that He hadn't forgotten them. The chapter opens with "Comfort, comfort my people," and that's exactly what these verses have become for me.

As I've wrestled with worry and exhaustion during this family health crisis, I keep coming back to one truth: He has been with us all our lives. This isn't the first difficult season we've navigated with God, and His past faithfulness becomes the foundation for trusting His promise of renewed strength today.

## The Promise That Meets Our Weakness

Here's what I love about Isaiah 40:29: "He gives power to the faint and weary, and to him who has no might He increases strength." God doesn't wait for us to get stronger before He helps us. He meets us in our weakness, in our exhaustion, in those moments when we honestly don't think we can handle one more piece of difficult news.

The cross-reference in my study Bible pointed me to 2 Corinthians 12:9: "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness." I'm discovering that my exhaustion and heartbreak aren't obstacles to God's strength—they're the very conditions where His strength shines brightest.

## Learning to Control What You Can

Perhaps the most practical lesson I'm learning through Isaiah 40:31 is about the freedom that comes from focusing on what I can actually control. When you're walking through a medical crisis with someone you love, there's so much that's completely out of your hands—test results, treatment outcomes, the timing of recovery or progression.

But here's what "waiting on the Lord" looks like in daily life: it's not passive sitting around. It's active trust that says, "God, You handle what only You can handle, and I'll handle what You've given me to handle."

I can't control the illness or the doctors' decisions, but I can control how I love my family member today. I can control whether I lean into God's strength or try to power through on my own. I can control my response to each new challenge, choosing trust over panic, presence over anxiety.

## The Lesson That's Still Coming

I'll be honest—I don't yet fully understand why this season has come or what God is ultimately teaching through it. But I'm learning to be okay with that. There's something powerful about being able to say, "I don't see it yet, but I trust the lesson will come clear in time."

Sometimes the most significant lessons in our spiritual lives come with a delay between the experience and the understanding. Right now, I'm in the "not yet" season of understanding, and that's okay. My job isn't to figure out God's complete plan—it's to receive the strength He's giving me for today.

## Walking Without Fainting

That daily strength—the kind that helps you show up when you feel empty, find words of comfort when your own heart is breaking, make decisions when your mind feels overwhelmed—that's the "walking without fainting" promise in action. It's not flashy or dramatic. It's just the quiet, steady power to keep loving, keep showing up, keep trusting, even when everything feels heavy.

The beautiful thing about God's promise in Isaiah 40:31 is that it meets us exactly where we are. Whether you need eagle's wings to soar above your circumstances, the energy to run toward new challenges, or simply the grace to keep walking through difficulty, God has strength specifically designed for your need.

For me, in this season of uncertainty and medical appointments and hard conversations, that strength looks like the ability to put one foot in front of the other. To trust the God who has been faithful all my life to continue being faithful, even when I can't see the full picture.

## The Wait That Renews

The Amplified Version's expansion of "wait" has become precious to me: "who expect, look for, and hope in Him." This isn't passive waiting—it's active expectation. It's looking for God's hand in the daily details, hoping in His character even when circumstances look dark.

As I write this, I still don't know how this chapter of our story will end. But I know the Author, and I trust that the same God who has carried us through every previous season will carry us through this one too. Some days that looks like soaring, some days like running, and some days like simply walking without fainting.

All three are gifts. All three are enough.

## God on the Mountain and in the Valley

There's a song by Lynda Randle called "God on the Mountain" that has been echoing in my heart during this journey. The song reminds us that while we often feel God's presence powerfully during our mountaintop experiences, He's equally present in the valleys of our lives. Sometimes it's easier to trust Him when everything is going well, but the real test—and the real growth—often comes in those valley seasons.

This health crisis with my family member has been a valley season, no doubt about it. But I'm discovering that God isn't any less present here than He was during the mountain moments of our lives. His strength shows up differently in the valleys—not as the triumphant "eagle's wings" feeling, but as that steady, sustaining "walking without fainting" grace that gets you through another difficult day.

The beauty of Isaiah 40:31's promise is that it acknowledges both realities: we need God on the mountain and in the valley. Whether we're soaring, running, or simply walking, His strength is perfectly suited to our need.

*If you're in your own "walking without fainting" season, may you find the same truth I'm discovering: God's strength isn't just available for the mountaintop moments. It's especially present in the valleys, in the waiting rooms, in the ordinary moments when you need grace just to keep going. He sees you. He knows what you need. And His strength is made perfect in your weakness.*
