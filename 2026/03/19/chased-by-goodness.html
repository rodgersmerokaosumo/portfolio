Chased by Goodness

March 19, 2026 2 min read

tags: Personal Reflections, GratitudeChased by Goodness

Psalm 23:6 - “Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life, and I will dwell in the house of the Lord forever.”

I was getting ready for bed last night and this verse stopped me.

Not because it’s new to me. I’ve read Psalm 23 more times than I can count. But something about reading it at the end of a long day, in the quiet before sleep, made it land differently. David isn’t asking for anything in this verse. He’s not pleading. He’s just… certain. Surely. As in, I already know this. As in, it’s settled.

I sat with that for a while.

The Hebrew word behind “follow” is radaph. It means to pursue. To chase down. It’s the same word used elsewhere in the Old Testament for enemies hunting someone. David takes that image and flips it completely. He’s saying: the thing in pursuit of me isn’t danger. It’s God’s goodness. God’s mercy. Running after me whether I notice or not.

That is a different way of moving through the world.

I think about my own life and I keep coming back to one thing: I work in a field I love. Data. Geospatial analysis. Agricultural systems. Impact measurement. Work that sits right at the crossroads of technology and people and the earth. I didn’t engineer my way into this - I followed what felt right, made decisions, took chances, and ended up somewhere that genuinely fits who I am.

For a long time I called that luck. Good timing. Being in the right place.

But the more I think about it, the more I believe I was being chased.

There’s a difference between finding your purpose and having your purpose find you. I think what happened to me is the second thing. The field chose me as much as I chose it. The curiosity was there before the career. The mind for patterns and systems and meaning in data - I didn’t develop that. I was given it. The family that holds me up when the work is heavy. The strength to keep going when the problems are hard. These aren’t things I manufactured.

They were given.

Verse 6 is the last line of the whole psalm. And I think that’s intentional. By the time you arrive here, David has already walked you through green pastures, dark valleys, tables set in front of enemies. He’s been through it. And on the other side of all of it, this is his conclusion: goodness and mercy were there the whole time. Even in the valley. Even when he couldn’t see it.

That’s the kind of faith I want. Not the kind that only shows up when things are easy. The kind that, after the hard seasons, looks back and says

  • oh. You were there.

I’m writing this at the end of a Thursday, about to sleep. Tomorrow there’s more work, more decisions, more problems that need careful thinking. And I’m not anxious about it.

Not because everything is perfect. But because I have evidence. A career I love. A family I don’t deserve. A mind that handles complexity I once thought was beyond me. A God who, apparently, has been running after me this whole time.

Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me.

I believe it.
